# Friction and Flow

*The History and Mystery of the Navier-Stokes Equations*

The story of the most important equations in fluid mechanics — derived five times independently, used in every aircraft and pipeline ever built, and still mathematically unresolved after 180 years. A narrative history for general readers, from Euler's frictionless ideal to a million-dollar unsolved problem.

## Contents

**Preface**

**Chapter 1 — The World Before Friction**
Euler's perfect fluid and why it fails in practice.

**Chapter 2 — The Engineer Who Got the Right Answer**
Claude-Louis Navier: bridge builder, molecular theorist, wrong for the right reasons.

**Chapter 3 — The Cambridge Mathematician**
George Gabriel Stokes: the Lucasian Chair, the continuum derivation, and Stokes's law.

**Chapter 4 — Five Men, One Equation**
The five independent derivations and Saint-Venant's fifty-year complaint.

**Chapter 5 — What the Equations Actually Say**
A term-by-term reading: pipe flow, the Reynolds number, laminar and turbulent regimes.

**Chapter 6 — The Monster Inside the Equations**
Turbulence: Reynolds's pipe experiment, Kolmogorov's cascade, and why CFD is always an approximation.

**Chapter 7 — A Million Dollars for a Proof**
The Clay Millennium Prize, finite-time blow-up, and the current state of the problem.
